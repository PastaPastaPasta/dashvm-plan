## 1. Acceptance restated

Kind `test`, tier xhigh, modules JOBS, INTEGRATION, ASSETS, section 6 of the parent plan, source row S06-26, workstream issue #4688. No capability requirements are recorded. The task ships when tests that drive the real block-execution entry points demonstrate all of the following, under the selected live-payment model (execution paid as it runs from the job's declared live payer, removal prepaid into the existing `40/128` prefunded ledger, no prepayment of future executions):

1. **Scheduling conservation.** After every block that contains scheduled work the credit conservation equation holds (`calculate_total_credits_balance().ok()`, the check `process_block_fees_and_validate_sum_trees_v0` already runs when `verify_sum_trees` is on), and the per-scenario deltas are exact: a successful attempt moves exactly its charge from the live payer to the processing pool (plus storage pool for any storage it wrote) and leaves the reserve untouched; a removal moves exactly the removal charge from the reserve to the processing pool and exactly the remainder to the recorded refund owner; nothing is credited twice and nothing is minted.
2. **Blocks with only scheduled work.** A block with zero transactions in which due jobs run advances the app hash, charges the live payer, lands fees in the current epoch's processing pool, and produces the same app hash on the proposing path and the validating path. The same holds on an epoch-boundary block (the DIP's "scheduled-only epoch-boundary blocks" vector): the charge lands in the new epoch's pool and the fee history is consulted for any refund.
3. **Bounded backlog processing.** With more due jobs than the per-block bound, exactly `bound` jobs run, in `(consensus_due_time, contract_id, job_id, occurrence)` order, the rest remain due with unchanged attempt counters and balances (skipping for capacity is not a paid attempt), the next block drains the next `bound`, a job whose admitted bound does not fit the block's remaining smart-contract computation is not admitted and not charged, and a job registered by an ordinary transition in block H is first eligible in block H+1.
4. **First-payment-failure removal.** When the live payer cannot cover the attempt bound at the due time: the guest is never invoked, the job and its due locator are removed, the removal charge is taken from the prepaid reserve, the remainder is refunded to the recorded owner, and the declared failure policy (Drop, Retry or Skip) plays no part. An empty live balance with a funded reserve is removed cleanly.
5. **Prepaid removal cost.** Registration debits the real funder by registration fee plus reserve, the reserve is visible under the prefunded root and counted by the conservation sum, a funder that cannot cover the reserve is rejected with no job, locator or reserve written, cancellation by the declared authority charges the bounded removal cost and refunds the rest to the recorded owner, cancellation by anyone else is rejected and changes nothing, a foreign funder's reserve returns to the foreign funder, and a refund owner that is a wiped contract routes to the current processing pool.
6. **Fee-table changes.** A job registered under protocol version P survives an upgrade to P+1 whose schedule raises the removal price: either its removal still succeeds funded by its reserve, or the upgrade settled it before raising an unpayable obligation. The test pins the invariant "no pre-upgrade job is left unremovable" on both proposal paths, and conservation holds across the epoch change.
7. **Each real attempt pays once (Q29).** A Retry job that fails deterministically pays one attempt charge per real attempt, its attempt counter increments once per attempt, the retry is due no earlier than the failure time plus the declared interval and is ineligible in the failing block, after `max_attempts` the `then` policy applies (Drop removes with the reserve; Skip advances), Drop removes on first failure, Skip advances a recurring job and completes a one-shot. Internal speculation adds no attempt, fee or receipt (on the serial path the invocation count equals the attempt count; the parallel path is R10-08's and R11-12's to extend).

Out of scope here: traps, exhaustion, freeze, unsupported versions and receipts on the scheduled path (R11-12), mixed direct/nested/recurring workloads and frozen behaviour (R10-08), scheduled host faults (FIX-03, merged design in #4808), the conservation helpers themselves (R12-10).

## 4. Test design for the resuming lane

### 4.1 Harness and fixtures

- **Chain level.** `packages/rs-drive-abci/tests/strategy_tests/test_cases/scheduled_job_tests.rs`, registered in `test_cases/mod.rs`. Every scenario runs with `independent_process_proposal_verification: true` (proposer and validator app hash parity on every block for free) and `verify_sum_trees: true`. Short clocks: `block_spacing_ms: 1_000`, `epoch_time_length_s: 60`, so an epoch boundary is reachable inside a 100-block chain. Seeded twins from FIX-03's helpers (lift them into `test_cases/abci_block_helpers.rs` if R04-04 has not already; both modules import them) for the one scenario where the validator must receive the block as gossiped rather than as prepared.
- **Unit level.** `packages/rs-drive-abci/src/execution/contracts/scheduling/tests.rs` (or the merged module path): the exact-delta matrix through `run_block_proposal` on a `TestPlatformBuilder` platform built the way FIX-03's `platform_with_scheduled_event_host_fault` builds it. Unit tests for the queue and reserve operations themselves stay with R06-09, R10-05 and R12-10.
- **Executor seam.** Before the runtime lands, R06-09 needs a stand-in for the JOBS to HOST call anyway. The tests use a `testing-config` outcome injection on that seam (requested from R06-09, section 10): per job id, `Success { consumed_units }` or `PaidFailure { consumed_units }`, plus an invocation counter. Once the runtime and call transition exist, the same tests run against a hand-written WebAssembly text fixture with a `succeed` and a `fail` export (R04-04's fixture style), and the seam is kept only for the "never invoked" assertion.
- **Registration and cancellation.** Through whatever ingress R06-09 and R10-05 ship: a native transition builder callable from the `strategy_tests` binary (preferred) or a host operation from the fixture guest. Adding `OperationType::ScheduleJob` and `CancelJob` to `packages/strategy-tests` is optional and only for random workloads; every scenario below is hand-built and deterministic.
- **Accessors the tests need** (requested from the code tasks): fetch job by id, fetch due locators in a time range, fetch a job's reserve balance and its recorded refund owner, fetch a job's attempt counter and next due time, and a way to read the per-block job bound and the removal price from the active platform version rather than a literal.

## 6. Provisional values and interpretations (every one goes in the PR body)

1. Per-block job bound: whatever R06-09 puts in `DriveAbciValidationConstants` (proposed name `maximum_scheduled_jobs_to_process`, register-adjacent to `maximum_vote_polls_to_process`); tests parameterise on it. No register row gives a number; the DIP only says "bounded".
2. Retry cap and interval: at most 10 attempts, interval at least one later block (register row "Scheduled retries"); G1 uses 3 to stay short.
3. Removal price: read from the fee schedule group R06-09 or R12-03 amends into `FEE_VERSION3`; the F1 mock schedule doubles it.
4. Prefunded purpose key for job reserves: allocation A20 (R12-10); FIX-09 took `129` for readiness, so `130` is the next free byte, but tests use the path helper and never the literal.
5. Order key `(consensus_due_time, contract_id, job_id, occurrence)` with the DIP's canonical encodings; C1 and C2 pin it.
6. Fee-upgrade policy: F1 pins the invariant (no pre-upgrade job left unremovable), not the mechanism; if R10-05 chooses "settle affected old jobs first", the boundary block must show the settlement and its refunds.
7. First-payment-failure removal is expected from R06-09 (section 6.4 policy); if it lands with R10-05 instead, D1 to D3 move to part 2.
8. "Scheduling conservation" means the equation plus exact per-scenario deltas; the equation's own term coverage is R12-10's.
9. Speculation assertions (G5) are serial-path only until the parallel executor exists.
